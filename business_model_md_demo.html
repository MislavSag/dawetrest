Business models

MD Demo business model

This way of looking at the business follows the layout from the Flourishing Business Canvas (FBC), which was explained earlier. Instead, it uses the FBC to make sure the new idea - the Green Hatchery - brings real benefits for nature, people, and money in the long run. But here, attention stays on how things stand right now. So there’s no jumping between different moments or timelines. What matters is today’s reality, not guesses about what might come later.

The Green Hatchery counts as a main outcome at the Middle Danube test area - seen as having strong market chances alongside solid development progress, likely hitting TRL 7 to 8. While it’s built for real-world use, its readiness stands out compared to similar projects in the region. Because of its advanced stage, it’s more than just an idea on paper - it works where it matters. Though still part of a demo setup, it already shows how green tech can move fast from concept to action.

We’ll display the entire business setup on a single canvas - yet go deeper into each part using step-by-step breakdowns.

2. Needs

The Needs module in the Flourishing Business Canvas (FBC) defines the critical problems, desires, or unmet requirements of the ecosystem actors that the enterprise is specifically designed to address or fulfill. Needs are fundamental because they provide the core rationale for the business’s existence; an Actor will only engage or become a Stakeholder if they believe the enterprise will help them meet one or more of their needs.

The Green Hatchery business is designed to meet critical, interconnected needs in the Middle Danube (MD) Demo region, from ecological preservation to socio-economic development.

Ecological and Environmental Needs

The core purpose of the DaWetRest project is the protection and restoration of wetlands and flood plains and their biodiversity. The Green Hatchery addresses specific ecological deficiencies in the MD Demo site:

  • Wetland and Habitat Restoration: There is an identified need to restore wetlands where approximately 80% of Europe’s wetlands have disappeared over the last 100 years, leading to a reduction in the number, size, and quality of marshes and lakes. The project seeks the revitalization of these areas, which is necessary for their future nature protection.
  • Biodiversity Support: The region needs an increased reserve of food (fish population) to support vulnerable and endangered waterfowls. This specific action is crucial to decrease the existing feeding pressure on the nearby protected area, the Kopački rit Nature Park.
  • Mitigation of Degradation Drivers: The wetlands need solutions to mitigate environmental threats, such as pollution and invasive species, which are exacerbated by intensive agriculture practices and climate change effects like altered precipitation patterns.

Community and Economic Needs

The Green Hatchery provides a critical tool for meeting local socio-economic needs, transforming environmental health into human prosperity and recreational value.

  • Recreation and Tourism Demand: Local residents highly value and actively utilize the area for leisure activities, including fishing, strolling, bird watching, and enjoying local festivities. There is a need to enhance and sustain these recreational amenities. The ecological revitalization provided by the hatchery and habitat restoration supports the development of sustainable tourism, particularly regenerative tourism with a focus on sport fisheries, to attract visitors and create local economic stability.
  • Need for local, healthy food supply: Local communities require access to safe and high-quality food resources. The Green Hatchery fulfills this need by enabling the availability of fresh, locally sourced fish, thereby contributing to food security.

3. Stakeholders

What is a Stakeholder, and Why are They Important?

A Stakeholder is an Actor (individual, group, or organization) who has a specific stake in the outcomes of the enterprise and assumes a defined role within or around it. They are more than just observers—they are engaged participants who believe their needs can be better fulfilled through the existence and activities of the business. In contrast to broader ecosystem actors, stakeholders are directly involved and necessary for the organization’s success.

In DaWetRest, stakeholders are classified using the Quadruple Helix (QH) model—Research & Academia, Governance, Business, and Civil Society—and also assessed based on their power/influence and interest. Following the consortium’s guidance, stakeholders are grouped as core, strategic, or potential.

Core Stakeholders (High Power & High Interest)

This group is critical for the success of the Green Hatchery and is actively engaged in development, operations, and decision-making.

  • Športsko-ribolovna udruga ODRA (ŠRU ODRA): Beneficiary partner and direct operator of the Green Hatchery complex. Responsible for local implementation and environmentally friendly aquaculture operations.
  • Adria Innovation Harbour (AIH): Leads the business model development for the MD Demo, especially the adaptation for SMEs. Participated in the opening and strategic planning.
  • UZ-FSB (Faculty of Mechanical Engineering and Naval Architecture, University of Zagreb): Provides technical and academic expertise. Plays a role in designing sustainable systems and aligning operations with ecological objectives.
  • Municipality of Draž: Local government responsible for permitting and community-level coordination. Supported the opening and operational launch.

Strategic Stakeholders (High Power & Low Interest)

These stakeholders have influence over funding, regulation, and policy but are less involved in daily activities.

  • Mission Ocean Secretariat / CINEA: Oversees project alignment with EU-level missions.
  • European Commission DG MARE: Demonstrated engagement by attending the Green Hatchery opening.
  • Ministry of Environmental Protection and Green Transition: Supports national-level alignment with biodiversity policy.
  • Regional Authorities: Oversee broader development frameworks and regulatory compliance.
  • G2O d.o.o.: Technology provider responsible for supplying and installing key aquaculture equipment. Supports technical feasibility and innovation.

Potential Stakeholders (Low Power & High Interest)

These actors are interested in outcomes and benefits, especially at the community level, but have limited direct power.

  • Local Businesses and Entrepreneurs: Target adopters of the SME-adaptable business model. Interested in creating revenue and jobs through sustainable aquaculture.
  • Protected Area Managers: Such as Javna ustanova Park prirode Lonjsko polje. Potential collaborators for biodiversity impact monitoring and conservation alignment.
  • Local Communities and General Public: Individuals and households near the site who benefit from improved ecological services, recreational use, and awareness.
  • Associated Regions (ARs): Replication partners applying DaWetRest models in other regions, particularly those receiving FSTP support from Bosnia & Herzegovina and beyond.

6. Value Co-Creations (Positive Outcomes)

The Green Hatchery is designed to co-create value across all sustainability pillars. Ecological Value is generated through enhanced biodiversity, specifically the production of 1200–1500 kg per year of indigenous fish, which reduces external feeding pressure on the Kopački rit Nature Park. The facility utilizes a green energy supply (solar panels and heat pumps) to manage operational costs sustainably. Economic Value is created by delivering an SME-adaptable business model that can be replicated, leading to increased local revenue and sustainable job creation in the aquaculture sector. Social Value includes knowhow transfer, stronger competencies and capacity building in the region, and new opportunities for development, resulting in “good restoration” in multiple ways.

7. Value Co-Destructions (Negative Outcomes/Mitigation)

Potential negative outcomes must be acknowledged and mitigated. Financial constraints pose a challenge due to the high initial capital cost for the specialized equipment (totaling approximately 116,195.00 EUR excluding PDV) and risks related to cost changes due to inflation and the economic situation. Regulatory challenges include potential delays in obtaining the necessary building permits. Environmental risks include the fact that the MD DEMO area is often flooded, which complicates field activities, and the ongoing background pressure from intensive agriculture practices in the region.

8. Products & Services

The core output is the Green Hatchery Complex itself (physical facility) located near the Topoljski Dunavac canal, classified as a commercial Key Exploitable Result (KER). This facility’s primary product is the cultivation and reproduction of indigenous fish populations (1200–1500 kg per year). Key intellectual products and services include the Green Hatchery Concept/Method (aiming for TRL 8), the replicable SME-adaptable Business Model (aiming for TRL 7), and the integrated Green Energy Supply system (heat pump/PVs) used for efficient operation and temperature control. The project also delivers data services, such as biodiversity monitoring reports (waterfowl, water, and soil quality data).

9. Partnerships

Partnerships are crucial for both implementation and scaling up the innovation. Core implementing partners include ŠRU Odra (site operations), UZ-FSB (technical R&D and data management), AIH (business strategy), HRVODE (executing hydro-technical works for ecosystem connectivity), and PSSS (providing essential water and soil quality analysis). Strategic partners include the Kopački rit Nature Park, which is interested in monitoring support. Collaboration with external experts like BRGM is necessary for adapting the carbon sequestration methodology, and engagement with sister projects facilitates knowledge exchange and capitalizing on synergies between DEMOs/results.

10. Governance

Governance ensures adherence to legal, ethical, and strategic frameworks. The project utilizes the Quadruple Helix model (integrating academia, government, business, and civil society) for comprehensive stakeholder engagement. The status of the Green Hatchery as a commercial innovation requires adherence to the IPR Management Strategy and continuous tracking in the Exploitation Logbook. Ethical compliance is maintained by adhering to the Ethics Management Plan and relevant national and EU laws, especially concerning the Natura 2000 status of the site. Internal monitoring of activities is conducted via the Master DEMO Status Monitoring and Registry Tool (D5.8), which is regularly updated by partners and validated by DEMO leaders for the Project Officer.

11. Resources

The GH relies on a combination of physical, financial, and intellectual assets. Physical resources include the GH complex, the specialized aquaculture equipment (tanks, pumps, incubation systems) costing approximately €116,195.00 (excluding PDV), and the solar panels and heat pumps which provide green energy supply and temperature control. Financial resources include the Horizon Europe grant funding and future potential investment from SMEs. Intellectual resources include the technical expertise from UZ-FSB (smart system design/energy optimization) and ŠRU Odra (aquaculture methods). Additionally, existing Backgrounds such as previous monitoring data, and methodologies (like the carbon sequestration methodology) are leveraged.

12. Activities

Activities are divided into active interventions and passive support. Active restoration measures include the installation and operation of the Green Hatchery for fish cultivation and reproduction (aiming for 1200–1500 kg/yr output) and the complementary hydro-technical works executed by HRVODE (removal of waterflow barriers). Passive/Support activities include continuous waterfowl monitoring (up to 3 times monthly by CEKOM), water/soil quality analysis (PSSS), and critical knowledge development actions such as the Development of a business model for the green hatchery (TRL 4 to 7 elevation) and the Strategy for the carbon sequestration capacity improvement (TRL 3 to 7 elevation).

13. Biophysical Stocks

These are the core natural resources that the Green Hatchery relies on and is intended to restore. These stocks include Water quality and supply within the Šarkanj canal and Lorencov Dunavac, regularly sampled and analyzed by PSSS. Soil stocks are also monitored for agrochemical properties, including humus, heavy metals, and organic carbon content, which is necessary for the Carbon Sequestration Strategy. The hatchery’s success depends on maintaining healthy Indigenous Fish Populations and supporting Wetland Vegetation which contributes to carbon sequestration potential.

14. Ecosystem Services

The project activities directly enhance several critical ecosystem services. The Green Hatchery operates as a Nature-Based Solution (NBS) for Biodiversity Enhancement and Habitat Restoration. The work on carbon sequestration potential contributes to Climate Mitigation. Improved hydrological conditions resulting from HRVODE’s actions contribute to Flood Regulation. The production of indigenous fish and the improved ecological status support Sustainable Tourism activities, such as regenerative sport fisheries.

15. Costs

The cost structure addresses financial needs and sustainability obligations. Financial costs primarily include the significant capital required for the specialized aquaculture equipment (€116,195.00 excl. PDV). Operational costs are minimized by the implementation of green energy systems (solar panels/heat pumps). Non-financial costs involve managing and mitigating the inherent risk of the demonstration site being often flooded, the continuous costs of specialized monitoring activities (waterfowl, water/soil), and the potential legal and administrative costs associated with securing the IPR for the commercial business model.

16. Goals

The overarching goal is to contribute to the protection and restoration of wetlands, floodplains, and their biodiversity within the Danube River Basin, specifically achieving a “good restoration” in multiple ways. Key technical goals involve elevating the TRL of the Green Hatchery Concept to TRL 8 and the Carbon Sequestration Strategy to TRL 7. Socio-economic goals center on promoting sustainable economic development through the implementation of a business model adaptable to SMEs and ensuring the enhancement of the interested area with the implemented activities proposed.

17. Benefits

The Green Hatchery delivers wide-ranging benefits. Environmental benefits include Biodiversity Preservation, Cleaner air and waters, and enhanced Climate Resilience. Economic benefits include generating Business nature based opportunities, increasing local incomes, and enabling the Sustainable economic development of the local community. Organizational and Social benefits include Knowhow transfer, Stronger competencies and capacity building across partners, enhanced organizational Reputation, and fostering new sustainable partnerships and stakeholder engagement, providing new opportunities for development.
